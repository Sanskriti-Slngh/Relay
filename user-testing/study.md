# P5: User Testing — Study Reports

## Study 1 — Participant 1

**Task duration:** 34 minutes  
**Assigned role:** Incoming MIT SAAS officer  
**Participant background:** Current MIT SAAS officer familiar with the challenges of sharing event-planning information across officers, both during a semester and during leadership transitions.  
**Facilitator assistance:** None during the seven tasks. A separate Context-update exercise was introduced afterward to investigate whether the initial difficulty persisted.

Participant 1 completed all seven tasks in 34 minutes. They opened the South Asian Formal Playbook to find the catering deadline, then followed its Context Note to identify other affected events. They also located and answered the outstanding guest question.

When recording the volunteer-lead decision, they initially added it to Formal's instructions. Once they considered Garba Night, they created a shared Context Note and attached it to both Playbooks. This suggests that the benefit of reuse became clear when a second application arose. However, the task's focus on a specific event may have encouraged their initial Playbook-first approach.

The participant also paused between "Fix wording" and "Information changed" when updating the catering deadline. During the debrief, they explained that both options seemed to edit the same text, making their different consequences unclear. This suggests a gulf of execution: the goal was understood, but the appropriate action was uncertain. In a separate follow-up exercise, they completed a comparable Context update without hesitation. Their improvement supports a first-use learning explanation, although recent exposure may account for the difference.

They subsequently corrected Formal and Garba Night and recognized that C-Show's existing 14-day deadline still satisfied the updated policy. Overall, the session suggests that Relay's shared-knowledge model can support reasoning across events, while the consequences of Context edits deserve clearer presentation to first-time officers.

## Study 2 — Participant 2

**Task duration:** 32 minutes  
**Assigned role:** Incoming MIT SAAS officer  
**Participant background:** New MIT SAAS member with limited experience managing the club's procedures. They represent newer members who may eventually take on officer responsibilities and need to understand decisions made by previous leadership.
**Facilitator assistance:** None during the seven tasks. A separate Step-reordering exercise was introduced afterward to investigate learnability.

Participant 2 completed all seven tasks in 32 minutes. Unlike Participant 1, they navigated directly to Shared Context to find the catering deadline and its connected Playbooks. This was notable because they had less experience with SAAS's existing procedures, suggesting that familiarity with the organization was not necessary to discover Relay's shared-knowledge structure. They answered the member's guest question using existing guidance, suggesting that the distinction between procedures and supporting information was understandable to them.

While improving Formal's dietary-restriction procedure, they added a Step at the end of the Playbook, then searched for a way to move it before the catering order. They eventually found the movement controls under the Step's additional-actions menu. This may reflect poor discoverability, but expectations formed by document editors are another plausible explanation. In a follow-up exercise at the end of the session, they reordered another Step without hesitation. That points toward a first-use difficulty, although the recent practice makes the result inconclusive.

They selected "Information changed" immediately and understood why multiple Reviews appeared, providing a useful contrast with Participant 1's hesitation.

Finally, they inspected C-Show's 14-day catering instruction twice before completing its Review without making edits. During the debrief, they explained that "Complete Review" initially sounded as though a correction should already have occurred. Their hesitation therefore suggests a gulf of evaluation: they understood that the procedure was still correct but were uncertain what the completion action would record.

The more important design opportunity appears to be clearer Review outcomes, while Step ordering may benefit from lightweight first-use guidance rather than a permanent redesign.
